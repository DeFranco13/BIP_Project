What is circularity
In today's global scenario, the transition to a Circular Economy is emerging as a crucial imperative for tackling the environmental and economic challenges facing our society. The Circular Economy is much more than a simple waste management approach, it represents a new business paradigm, where resources are used efficiently and regeneratively, while products and materials circulate continuously through various processes, such as maintenance, reuse, renovation, remanufacturing, recycling and composting.

This innovative business model, with its emphasis on sustainability, is becoming increasingly recognized for its potential to generate competitiveness and create a positive impact on the global economy. To successfully implement the Circular Economy, a change in the traditional approach towards the market, customers and natural resources is crucial. This not only promotes cost reduction, but also the efficient use of energy, the reduction of CO2 emissions and the establishment of more optimized and secure supply chains.

The Circular Economy is driving a restructuring of the economic model by coordinating production and consumption systems in closed circuits. This dynamic process requires not only technical and economic compatibility (skills and productive activities), but also an appropriate social and institutional context (incentives and values).


The Circular Economy transcends the narrow confines of waste management and recycling efforts, aspiring to a wider range of initiatives. These include reformulating processes, products and new business models, as well as optimizing the use of resources. The aim is to effectively "circulate" products, components and materials in technical and/or biological cycles. This approach promotes the creation of economically sustainable and ecologically efficient products and services. It manifests itself through strategies such as reducing resource extraction, maximizing reuse, increasing efficiency and innovating new business paradigms.
